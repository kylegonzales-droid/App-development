# Kingston upon Thames — geographic research

Research notes gathered for Phase 1 world design. **This is reference material for
building an original stylised world.** No map data, tiles, imagery or database from
any mapping provider has been copied into this project, and none may be.

Sources are public encyclopaedic and local-interest pages; `en.wikipedia.org`,
`kingston.gov.uk` and `sabre-roads.org.uk` were blocked by this environment's
egress proxy, so facts below come from search result summaries and the pages that
were reachable. Anything marked *(unverified)* needs a second source before it
drives a design decision.

## The river and its crossings

- **River Thames** runs roughly north–south along the town's western edge.
- **Kingston Bridge** carries the **A308** across the Thames. Stone arch, five
  spans, four piers in water, opened 17 July 1828, designed by Edward Lapidge.
  West bank is **Hampton Wick**.
- **Kingston Railway Bridge** crosses the Thames north of the road bridge.
- **Hogsmill River** — 9.9 km, rising at Bourne Hall Park in Ewell, joining the
  Thames *in Kingston*. It enters the town from the south-east. This gives the
  town centre a second, much smaller watercourse and a green corridor.
- **Clattern Bridge** carries traffic over the Hogsmill near the Guildhall.
  Built around 1175 — one of the oldest intact bridges in England. The name
  (recorded as *Clateryngbrugge*) is thought to come from the clatter of hooves.
- **Canbury Gardens** — 14½ acres in the Canbury district, along **Lower Ham
  Road**, between the road and the Thames towpath, *downstream* of the railway
  bridge. Riverside pub on the terrace.

## The old core

- The **Old Town** retains an essentially **medieval street layout**. Building
  plots around All Saints' churchyard, the Apple Market, and the eastern sides of
  the Market Place and Church Street keep medieval widths — as narrow as 3 m,
  typically 5–8 m.
- **Market Place** — focal point for 800+ years, still an active market.
- **All Saints' Church** — 14th century.
- **Old Town Hall** — Victorian. **Church Street** — Georgian. **Market Place** —
  neo-Tudor frontages. Architecturally very mixed.
- **Clarence Street** was cut as the new approach to Kingston Bridge.
- Late-Victorian and 20th-century development merged many narrow plots, especially
  on the west side of Market Place and on **Thames Street**.

## The one-way system — the most gameplay-relevant real feature

- The **Kingston Relief Road**, universally called the **one-way system**, was
  built in **1989**. The main shopping streets were **pedestrianised** and traffic
  pushed onto a one-way ring around the centre.
- Its centrepiece is the crossroads where **Eden Street, Richmond Road, Cromwell
  Road and Wood Street** converge.
- On **Wood Street** the system descends a ramp, squeezes under a second railway
  bridge, and forms an odd triangular junction with the **dual-carriageway A308**
  heading for Kingston Bridge.
- Two separate bus stations — **Cromwell Road** and **Fairfield** — physically
  split by the one-way system.
- Proposals exist to return parts to two-way working *(unverified, and irrelevant
  to a game set now)*.

**Design consequence:** a pedestrianised core encircled by a fast one-way ring is
an unusually good game map. It separates on-foot and vehicle play spatially,
gives chases a natural circuit, and makes "wrong way round the loop" a
locally authentic transgression.

## Rail

- **Kingston station** and **Norbiton station** both run to London Waterloo —
  either via New Malden and Wimbledon, or via Richmond.
- **Surbiton station**, to the south, is the fast one: roughly every 15 minutes,
  under 20 minutes to Waterloo.
- A railway viaduct crosses the town; the arches under it are a real and very
  British urban texture (garages, trade counters, storage).

## Retail and public space

- **Bentall Centre** — large covered shopping centre, opened 1992, on the site of
  a department store established 1867. 75+ stores.
- **Eden Walk** — open precinct shopping centre, opened 1968.
- **Charter Quay** — riverside leisure/residential development.
- **Ancient Market Place** — markets including seasonal continental and Christmas.
- Rooftop bars overlooking the Market Place; riverside pubs.

## Districts and surroundings

- **Canbury** (north, riverside), **Norbiton** (east), **Surbiton** (south),
  **Hampton Wick** (west, over the bridge), **New Malden** (east),
  **Ham** (north).
- **Kingston University** has four campuses: **Penrhyn Road**, **Kingston Hill**,
  **Knights Park** (riverside, School of Art), **Roehampton Vale**. A large
  student population is a defining feature of the town's night-time character.

## Real residential estates — explicit exclusion

The borough's largest regeneration scheme is a real, occupied council estate in
Norbiton, bounded by three named real roads, being rebuilt in five phases.

**It must not be used, named, mapped, or recognisably depicted in this game.**
Real people live there. Depicting a real, identifiable, occupied estate as gang
territory is defamatory to its residents, and no amount of renaming fixes it if
the geometry and location still identify the place.

The game's estate is **fictional**, placed in a **fictional pocket** of the map
that corresponds to no real estate, and is written with residents as people
rather than scenery. See `docs/design/01-world-and-map.md`.

## What we take, and what we leave

**Take — geographic relationships that make Kingston feel like Kingston:**
river on the west; a single dominant road bridge; a railway bridge just upstream;
a towpath running north; a small tributary joining from the south-east under an
ancient low bridge; a medieval pedestrianised core of narrow plots; a fast
one-way ring around that core; a covered mall pressed against the old market;
a viaduct with arches; two commuter stations; a large student population;
riverside greens to the north.

**Leave — anything owned by someone:**
real business names and branding, real estate and street identities used
pejoratively, mapping-provider tiles, imagery, labels, styling or data, real
people, and real criminal organisations.
