# Protagonist

## Remi Osei-Turner

**Age** 24. **From** Greenside, Kingston — Block C, still lives there.
**Pronouns** they/them. *(See "Character options" below — this is a deliberate
choice with production consequences, and it is yours to confirm or change.)*

### Who they are

Mixed Ghanaian-English. Grew up on the estate, went to school in the borough,
has left the M25 four times. Was two years into an electrical apprenticeship at
the college when their mum's MS got bad enough to need someone at home in the
afternoons, and the apprenticeship quietly became impossible. Now drives nights
for a courier app and does cash-in-hand work at **Verrall's Motors** in Ashdown
Works — recovery, jump-starts, moving cars that need moving.

**The skill that matters:** Remi knows the geography of Kingston better than
anyone alive. Not the streets — the *joins*. Which service door is unlocked until
eleven. Which bollard drops for the market van at six. Which car park deck the
camera doesn't reach. Which culvert comes out behind the allotments. Four years of
night deliveries in a town with a pedestrianised centre and a one-way ring will
teach you that, and it is the single thing both factions actually want.

They are **not** a fighter. They are a driver, a fixer, and a person everyone
tells things to.

### Circumstances at the start

- Living at home. Mum, **Adwoa**, 54, MS, good days and bad days.
- Older sister **Dami**, 29, manages **The Late Plate** — a chicken and grill
  shop on the corner where the Crossing comes out. Open till three. Both crews eat
  there. Neither has ever caused trouble in it. It is the closest thing Kingston
  has to neutral ground, and Dami has kept it that way by sheer force of
  personality.
- About £400 in the bank. Rent is fine this month. Next month is a question.
- Known to both factions as *that courier who knows the back ways*, and trusted by
  both precisely because they have never picked a side.

### Personality

Dry. Understated. Deflects with humour, usually at their own expense. Listens more
than they talk, which is why people tell them things. Genuinely likeable and
genuinely evasive. Notices everything and comments on almost none of it.

**Core flaw: Remi cannot say no.** Not from weakness — from a real, decent
instinct to keep everyone alright and nobody upset. It is exactly how you end up
working for both sides of a war. Every disaster in the story traces back to a
moment where Remi said "yeah, alright" when they should have said no.

**Core strength:** calm. Remi is the person who does not panic — in a car, in a
stairwell, in a conversation that is going badly. That is the player fantasy made
character.

### Motivation

Three, in order of how honest Remi is about them:

1. **Stated:** money. Rent, mum's care, keeping the lights on.
2. **Actual:** keeping The Late Plate neutral. It's Dami's, it's the last decent
   thing on that corner, and both crews leaning on it is unbearable.
3. **Unadmitted:** Remi liked being the person both sides trusted. Being needed by
   everyone felt like being someone. Losing that is the real fear.

### Voice

South-west London. Understated, specific, unshowy. Says "mate" and "you alright?"
and "nah, listen". Never announces anything. Never threatens. Deflects
compliments. Swears when genuinely rattled, which is rare and therefore lands.

**Sample — being leaned on by Col Marsh:**

> **COL:** You know the yard, you know the timings, you know the ramp.
> **REMI:** I know a lot of ramps.
> **COL:** Don't be clever.
> **REMI:** I'm not being clever, Col, I'm being slow. There's a difference and
> you'll notice it in a minute.

**Sample — with Dami:**

> **DAMI:** Who was that?
> **REMI:** Bloke about a van.
> **DAMI:** Remi.
> **REMI:** He was. He was about a van. It's just also about other things.

**Never:** American crime-game cadence. No "let's do this", no speeches about
respect, no declaring that they run anything. If a line would sound at home in a
Los Angeles crime game, it is wrong.

### Visual identity

**Silhouette first** — must read at 64 px on a phone from a high-angle camera.

- **Build:** average height, wiry, slightly hunched from four years on a bike.
- **Head:** short twists under a beanie in cold, bare in summer. Beanie is the
  primary silhouette read.
- **Body:** the signature is the **courier bag** — a large square thermal-ish
  satchel across the back, and it never comes off. It makes the silhouette
  unmistakable from above, which is exactly what a top-down game needs.
- **Clothing:** black or charcoal technical jacket, slightly too big, reflective
  piping on the sleeves that catches headlights. Dark jeans. Battered trainers.
  Layered, practical, cheap, real. No branding of any kind.
- **Colour:** deliberately **neutral** — black, charcoal, one flash of warm
  orange on the bag strap. Remi wears neither faction's colours. When the story
  forces a choice, giving them a green or hi-vis item is the visual beat.

**Animation notes:** hands in pockets at idle. Checks phone constantly. Runs
like someone who runs a lot, not like an athlete. Vaults low obstacles without
ceremony.

### Arc

**Act I — Yes.** Remi takes work from both sides because both ask nicely and the
money is good. Learns the town is worth something to people. Enjoys it.

**Act II — Held.** The double game becomes load-bearing. Both sides start asking
for things that hurt the other. The Late Plate gets leaned on. Remi's calm starts
looking like cowardice to everyone who cares about them — especially Mikey.

**Act III — No.** Remi finds out about Toyin: Quayside's nine-month maintenance
fault *and* Sanaa's eleven grand. There is no side. The finale is Remi, for the
first time, refusing everyone — and having to make that stick in a town where
they now have no protection from anyone.

The last scene is not a crowning. It's the shutter going up at The Late Plate.

### Character options — a decision for you

I have written Remi as non-binary and used they/them. It is not a neutral choice
and you should make it deliberately:

| Option | Pull | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **As written (they/them)** | Genuinely uncommon in the genre; the "person everyone talks to" reading is strong; modern SW London is authentically diverse | Needs careful writing to avoid tokenism; some markets react badly; VO casting narrower |
| **Female (she/her)** | Also rare in this genre; the calm-driver fantasy works identically | Nothing structural changes |
| **Male (he/him)** | Genre default, lowest friction | Least distinctive; risks reading as generic |
| **Player choice at start** | Widest appeal | Doubles VO cost, weakens a specific authored character |

**My recommendation:** keep a single authored protagonist rather than a player
choice — this story depends on a specific person with a specific flaw — but the
gender is genuinely yours. Nothing else in the design changes.
