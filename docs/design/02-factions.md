# Factions and the conflict

Both factions are **entirely fictional**. They are not based on, named after, or
modelled on any real organisation, real Kingston group, or any real gang
anywhere. No real gang iconography, colours or terminology is used.

## The conflict — why it actually started

Not hatred. **A partnership that worked, and a liability nobody would accept.**

For three years, **Yvonne Marsh** (Quayside) and **Sanaa Hassan** (Five Star) had
an arrangement. Quayside supplied and stored; Five Star distributed through the
legitimate delivery network. It was efficient, quiet, and nearly bloodless. Both
made more money than either had alone.

It broke over **the Crossing** — the loading yard → arch → car park → ramp chain
in Zone C. Quayside holds the yard lease. Five Star knows the timing.

**The incident.** A wet night in February. A Five Star rider, **Toyin Fenn**, 22,
was told the ramp was clear. It was not — a Quayside van was reversing blind on
the lower deck with a broken reversing camera that had been on the maintenance
list for nine months. Toyin died on the ramp.

**What each side did next.** Quayside called it an accident and paid nothing —
because paying anything would be conceding that the route was theirs to manage,
and the lease is the only real asset they have. Five Star called it negligence
and stopped working with them.

**What neither side admits.** Sanaa knew the ramp was unsafe. She had flagged it
twice and sent Toyin anyway, because that run was worth eleven thousand pounds.
The player learns this roughly two-thirds through — and at that point there is no
side left to be on.

So: **a liability dispute between two organisations who were worth more as
partners, escalating because an apology would cost each of them the one thing
they cannot give up.** Territory, money and reputation are all downstream of that.

## FACTION A — QUAYSIDE

*Old Kingston. Riverside. Family. Convinced they are the legitimate order.*

**Identity.** Three generations of market traders and hauliers. They do not think
of themselves as criminals; they think of themselves as the people who have
always run the practical business of this town — storage, haulage, van hire,
market pitches, event security — and who occasionally move things they shouldn't.
They talk about the **charter**: a self-serving myth that the market's ancient
rights descend to whoever holds the pitches, meaning them.

**Colour language.** Deep bottle green, oxidised copper, brass, weathered timber,
oxblood. River colours. Nothing high-vis. Nothing new.

**Symbol.** A **swan**, in profile, on a plain green field — painted on van doors,
stencilled on storage units, tattooed badly on older members. On the Thames the
swans belong to the Crown by ancient right. The joke, and the threat, is
ownership by inheritance.

**Base.** Marsh Self-Store & Van Hire, service road behind the boatyard (Zone B).

**Strengths.** Property, leases, keys, storage, a magistrate's-clerk knowledge of
what is technically legal, thirty years of favours, genuine standing among older
residents.

**Weaknesses.** Authority is inherited, not earned by this generation. They are
slow. They cannot recruit under twenty-five. Everything they own is a physical
asset that can be seized, burned or leaked.

### People

**Yvonne Marsh, 58 — principal.**
Runs the storage yard. Never raises her voice, never swears, remembers every
name. Reads the local paper cover to cover. Believes absolutely that she is a
businesswoman who has kept this town's practical life running while nobody
thanked her. She did not order Toyin's death and is genuinely sorry about it —
and will still not pay, because the lease is everything. *Arc:* her refusal to
concede a single inch is what destroys the thing she built.

**Col Marsh, 34 — her nephew, lieutenant.**
Impatient, reasonably bright, wants to modernise, resents being passed over for a
woman he thinks is thirty years out of date. Not stupid, not a thug — he is right
that the old model is dying, and wrong about everything he wants to replace it
with. *Arc:* his attempt to force the issue is the mid-game escalation.

**Denise Ahluwalia, 61 — the books.**
Bookkeeper, has worked for the Marshes for twenty-two years. Knows precisely how
exposed they are. The only person Yvonne listens to, and increasingly the only
one telling her the truth. *Arc:* the potential defector.

**Ordinary members.** Yard hands, van drivers, market traders paying for a pitch
and a bit of protection they mostly don't need. Most have no idea how bad it has got.

### Internal conflict
Yvonne wants to hold. Col wants to strike. Denise wants to settle and pay.
All three are talking to Remi separately, and none of them knows it.

## FACTION B — FIVE STAR

*Young. Distributed. No territory — logistics. Named for the delivery rating.*

**Identity.** Grew out of the gig economy, not the street. Couriers, riders,
night-shift warehouse staff. They have no turf in the old sense; they have
**routes, timings and coverage**. Product moves inside real deliveries, on real
apps, in real livery, past real cameras, entirely in plain sight. Everyone is a
contractor. Nobody knows more than their own leg of the chain.

They find Quayside's charter mythology genuinely ridiculous — a family cosplaying
as medieval burghers while a spreadsheet eats their business.

**Colour language.** High-visibility yellow-green, matte black, reflective silver,
the grey-blue of a phone screen at night. Livery colours. Institutional, cheap,
everywhere, invisible.

**Symbol.** A **five-pointed rating star** — the one from a delivery app review —
rendered flat in hi-vis. Stickered on lampposts, thermal bags, e-bike battery
cases. The irony is the point.

**Base.** No headquarters. A lock-up on the Greenside garage row is the nearest
thing to a dispatch point, and it changes every few weeks.

**Strengths.** Speed, coverage, deniability, no physical assets to seize, a
genuine technical edge in the routing system, and a workforce that is legitimately
employed doing a legitimate job most of the time.

**Weaknesses.** No loyalty — everyone is a contractor and can be outbid. No
enforcement capacity beyond a few angry twenty-year-olds. The whole structure
depends on one routing system and one person's head. Grief is running the
organisation right now, and grief is a bad operations director.

### People

**Sanaa Hassan, 27 — "Ops". Coordinator, not leader.**
Former logistics planner for a real distribution firm; quit, or was pushed. Never
touches product, never meets a supplier, has no criminal record and intends to
keep it that way. Runs the network from a dispatch board. Speaks in capacity,
coverage and exceptions. Her power is a routing system she wrote and will not
share. *Arc:* she sent Toyin knowing the ramp was bad. She has recast that
decision, to herself, as Quayside's fault — and she is building a war to keep
from examining it. The truth coming out is the third-act turn.

**Mikey Fenn, 19 — Toyin's younger brother. Best rider in Kingston.**
Fearless, genuinely gifted on a bike, reckless, and cannot stop posting. Wants to
be known. Grieving badly and channelling it into escalation. The most likeable
person in the faction and the most dangerous. *Arc:* either Remi pulls him out of
this or he does something that cannot be undone. This is the game's strongest
optional relationship.

**Bev Oyelaran, 31 — the one with a conscience.**
Does two legitimate jobs and this. Has a kid. Wants out, needs the money, keeps
setting a leaving date and moving it. The player's most reliable source of honest
information. *Arc:* the cost of the conflict, measured in one ordinary life.

**Ordinary members.** Riders and drivers, many of whom genuinely do not know what
is in the bag, and are furious when they find out.

### Internal conflict
Sanaa wants a clean takeover of the route. Mikey wants someone to pay for his
sister. Bev wants it to stop. Nobody is in charge enough to decide.

## Neither side is good

| | Defensible | Indefensible |
|---|---|---|
| **Quayside** | They genuinely built something. They employ people. They didn't mean to kill anyone. | They let a maintenance fault stand for nine months and refused to pay a penny to a dead girl's family, for a lease. |
| **Five Star** | Their people are exploited by everyone, including the legitimate economy. Toyin's death was preventable. | Sanaa knew, and sent her anyway, for eleven grand. Now she's using the grief as a takeover instrument. |

The player should be able to finish the game and conclude, honestly, that the
right answer was for both of them to lose.

## Reputation model

Two **independent** scalars, not one slider: `standing.quayside` and
`standing.fiveStar`, each −100…+100. You can be trusted by both, hated by both,
or anywhere between. Holding both is lucrative, unstable, and the actual
dramatic engine of the story. See `06-mission-system.md`.
