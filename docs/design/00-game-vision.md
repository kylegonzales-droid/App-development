# Game vision

## Title

**Recommended: `MANOR`**

"Round my manor" is British slang for *my local area, my patch* — and a manor is
also an inherited estate held by ancient right. Both meanings are the game. Short,
unmistakably British, no American reading, works as an app icon wordmark.

| Alternate | Reasoning | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **CROWN STONE** | Kingston = "King's town"; Saxon kings were crowned here. About who gets crowned locally. | Reads fantasy at a glance |
| **CLATTERN** | From the 1175 bridge over the Hogsmill; sounds like violence, is pure Kingston | Meaningless outside the borough |
| **LOW WATER** | River, and being at a low ebb | Generic |
| **FIVE STAR** | The delivery-rating irony (see Faction B) | Better as the faction name |

`MANOR` is a common English word, so App Store search is a real concern. Ship it
with a locked subtitle — **`MANOR: Kingston`** — for store metadata and keep the
wordmark as `MANOR`.

## One-line pitch

> A courier who knows every back route in Kingston gets caught between the two
> crews fighting over the only one that matters.

**Longer:** *Kingston upon Thames, now. Remi Osei-Turner drives nights and knows
the town's service yards, arches and loading bays better than anyone. When an old
arrangement between the riverside's oldest family firm and the town's delivery
network collapses over a death nobody will take responsibility for, both sides
want the same thing from Remi: the route. Neither is telling the truth about what
happened, and the one neutral place left in town is Remi's sister's takeaway —
on the corner where that route comes out.*

## Player fantasy

**"I know this town better than anyone chasing me."**

Not *I am the most dangerous person here*. Remi is not the hardest, best armed, or
most feared. The fantasy is **mastery of place**: the shortcut through the
culvert, the service door that's unlocked until eleven, the car park deck where
the camera doesn't reach, the fact that the one-way system means the police car
behind you has to go the long way round.

Corollaries that shape every system:
- **Knowledge beats force.** Escape and misdirection are always viable.
- **The map is the weapon.** Learning geography is the real progression curve.
- **Being trusted is a resource** — and spending it costs something.

## Core game loop

**Moment (seconds).** Move, read the street, pick a line. Drive, dodge, talk.

**Job (3–8 minutes).**
```
   Get a job  ──►  Travel there   ──►  Do the thing  ──►  It goes wrong
   (phone)         (the route is      (deliver, take,     (a complication
                    the gameplay)      talk, follow)       fires)
        ▲                                                       │
        │                                                       ▼
   Standing, money,  ◄──  Get clear  ◄──  Choose how to handle it
   world state             (evade, lose heat, go quiet)
```

**Session (20–40 minutes).** Two or three jobs, a story beat, a change in the
town: a shutter down that was open, a crew on a corner that was empty.

**Campaign.** Standing with two factions moves *independently*. You are not
sliding along one bar between them — you can be trusted by both, or burned by
both. The story is about how long you can hold that, and what it costs when you
can't.

## Design pillars

Every feature is tested against these four. A feature that serves none is cut.

1. **Kingston is a character.** If a system could be lifted into any city
   unchanged, it is not finished. The one-way system, the towpath, the arches,
   the river fog, the rhythm of the last train — these are mechanics, not dressing.
2. **Understated, not swaggering.** British crime drama, not American power
   fantasy. Dialogue is dry, deflecting, specific. Nobody announces they run this
   town. The tension is in what is not said.
3. **Nobody is clean.** Both factions have a defensible position and an
   indefensible act. The player is allowed to conclude that neither deserves to win.
4. **The thumb is the whole interface.** Designed for one hand on a phone on a
   bus, not a controller shrunk down.

## What this game is not

- Not photorealistic. Not trying to be.
- Not a shooter. Firearms are rare, frightening, and escalate the story
  permanently when used. Most conflict is avoided, driven away from, or talked down.
- Not gory. Stylised, bloodless-to-restrained impact.
- Not a parody of British life, and not poverty tourism.
